Decisions that matter deserve more than a single model's guess.

Quorum is a structured multi-agent deliberation engine. Instead of one AI producing an answer, a team of AI agents with distinct expert perspectives argue, challenge, and synthesize through formal debate protocols — producing not just a recommendation, but a complete, auditable reasoning record.

"You don't have to trust AI to give you the right answer. You have to be able to trust the process that produced the answer."
AI is being embedded in consequential decisions at an accelerating pace: clinical care pathways, enterprise architecture, legal risk assessment, product strategy, compliance review. And yet, adoption stalls at a predictable inflection point — the moment when the humans responsible for a decision are asked to accept an AI's recommendation on something that really matters.
The reason is not distrust of AI in general. It is distrust of the black box. A single model ingests a prompt and returns a conclusion. There is no record of what it considered, what it rejected, what objections it encountered. The output exists without a history.
For consequential decisions, that absence is disqualifying. Quorum exists to close this gap — by making the process visible, the reasoning auditable, and the dissent documented.
The Mechanism
Three rounds. Multiple perspectives. One auditable record.
Every Quorum deliberation follows an invariant structure. The agent roster is configurable — 3 for a quick analysis, 9 for a high-stakes decision with regulatory implications — but the process is always the same.
ROUND 1
Specialist Analysis
Each agent analyzes the problem from its domain perspective: technical feasibility, legal risk, user impact, security implications, business value, cost, timeline. Agents do not simply agree — they each bring the concerns and constraints native to their discipline. Every position is recorded.
Engineering Liaison System Architect Security Officer Legal Specialist UX Designer Stakeholder Advocate
ROUND 2
Skeptic Challenge
A designated Skeptic agent reviews all specialist input and systematically challenges the reasoning. It questions assumptions, demands evidence, identifies compounding risks, probes for overconfidence, and ensures the team has not rushed toward consensus. The Skeptic's challenges become part of the permanent record — every objection raised, whether sustained or overcome.
Skeptic Agent
ROUND 3
Synthesis & Recommendation
A synthesis agent resolves or acknowledges unresolved tensions, assigns a calibrated confidence score (0.0–1.0), flags whether the decision should proceed automatically or escalate for human review, and produces the final recommendation — along with a structured record of what was decided, what was contested, what was explicitly not chosen, and why.

The Output
A decision artifact, not just a recommendation.
Every Quorum deliberation produces a complete, permanent record.
Decision Summary
What was decided, at what confidence level, and whether it proceeds autonomously or requires human review.
Full Debate Transcript
Verbatim, attributed exchange. Every perspective recorded. Every challenge documented. Permanently preserved.
Alternatives Record
Every path considered and rejected, with explicit reasoning for why it was not taken. The roads not traveled matter.
Confidence Score
0.0–1.0 calibrated to the quality of reasoning, not the certainty of the model. A 0.65 with three open concerns is more honest than a 0.95 from a black box.
Escalation Signal
When confidence falls below threshold, Quorum creates a human-in-the-loop request with exactly the context needed to exercise judgment.
Structured Documents
Architecture docs, decision summaries, work plans, risk registers — generated from deliberation content, not boilerplate.
Debate Patterns
Different stakes call for different processes.
Quorum supports multiple deliberation structures. The pattern is selected based on the stakes, time available, and nature of the problem.
Standard
3 rounds · 5–9 agents · Most decisions
The core three-round structure: specialist analysis, skeptic challenge, synthesis. Suitable for feature decisions, architecture reviews, risk assessments, and most consequential choices.
Deep Deliberation
5 rounds · 7–12 agents · High stakes
Extended structure with rebuttal and reassessment rounds. Agents revisit and revise positions after hearing challenges. Used when initial confidence is low or when the decision has irreversible consequences.
Red Team
Adversarial · 2 teams · Security & architecture
One team argues for a proposal. Another is tasked with defeating it. Surfaces hidden weaknesses, attack vectors, and failure modes before commitment. Essential for security-sensitive and infrastructure decisions.
Six Thinking Hats
Parallel perspectives · 6 modes · Strategy
De Bono's methodology: each agent adopts a specific thinking mode — facts, emotion, caution, optimism, creativity, process — rather than its domain role. Particularly effective for strategy, innovation, and product direction questions.
Speed Panel
Compressed · 3 agents · Lower stakes
Rapid deliberation for time-sensitive or lower-stakes decisions. Fewer rounds, smaller roster, faster turnaround. Still produces a transcript and confidence score — just leaner.
Multi-Stage Chain
Sequential stages · Context inheritance
Complex projects move through stages where each stage's output becomes input to the next. The agent roster differs per stage, but accumulated reasoning carries forward. The architecture team knows why requirements were scoped as they were.
Where It Applies
Any domain where the reasoning matters as much as the result.
Quorum is domain-agnostic by design. The agent roster is configured per workflow. These are the domains where the trust gap is most acute.
🏥
Clinical Decisions
Care pathways, treatment protocols, diagnostic reasoning. Where the decision affects patient outcomes and the rationale must be defensible.
Trust need: Clinicians cannot justify a decision by citing "the AI said so." They can justify a structured analysis where competing considerations were weighed.
⚖️
Legal Risk Assessment
Contract analysis, regulatory interpretation, litigation risk, compliance review. Where the reasoning is the deliverable, not just the conclusion.
Trust need: Legal counsel needs to see that risk was debated, mitigated, and documented — not summarized by a single model pass.
🏗️
Architecture & Engineering
System design, technology selection, infrastructure planning. Where trade-offs are genuine and the wrong call compounds for years.
Trust need: Engineering teams trust architectures more when they see that security, scalability, and maintenance concerns were represented in the deliberation.
📊
Financial & Investment
Capital allocation, risk assessment, portfolio strategy. Where quantitative analysis meets qualitative judgment under uncertainty.
Trust need: Investment committees must document their process. A debate transcript with confidence scores is a compliance artifact.
🛡️
Compliance & Ethics
GDPR, HIPAA, CCPA determinations. Data use ethics. Where the question is not just "can we?" but "should we?" and the answer must be defensible.
Trust need: Compliance decisions need to survive regulatory audit. The record of how the decision was reached matters as much as the decision itself.
🚀
Product Strategy
Prioritization, market entry, feature strategy. Where trade-offs are real — engineering effort, market timing, user value, competitive position.
Trust need: Product decisions require buy-in from multiple functions. A strategy debated by engineering, UX, and market perspectives earns that buy-in faster.
